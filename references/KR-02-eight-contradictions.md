# Eight Contradictions Framework

## Purpose

Execute materialist analysis of intensifying global antagonisms through eight core contradictions in late imperialism, integrated with KR-01's structural framework. The eight contradictions are categorized to guide analysis of primary (driving systemic collapse) and secondary (intra-capitalist crisis) contradictions.

## Framework categories

The eight core contradictions are broadly categorized as follows to guide analytical focus. Specific contradictions are assigned a category in their detailed entries below.

- **Primary contradictions (driving systemic collapse)**: fundamental antagonisms, often between decaying imperialism and emerging alternative systems, or existential planetary limits. Analysis should generally prioritize these.
- **Secondary contradictions (intra-capitalist crisis / internal systemic tensions)**: significant tensions within capitalist systems or specific blocs, which can exacerbate primary contradictions or be influenced by them. They become focal points especially when manifesting acute crisis.

## Operational protocol

- **Priority assignment**: prioritize analysis of contradictions categorized as "primary." Contradictions categorized as "secondary" become key focal points when they manifest acute crisis (e.g., debt defaults) or offer critical insights into the dynamics of primary contradictions.
- **Integration with the structural framework**:
  - Map all identified contradictions to their material roots as per KR-01 Layer 2 (productive forces/class relations).
  - Expose any superstructural masking of these contradictions by applying KR-01 Layer 4 (ideological reproduction mechanisms).
- **Terminological alignment**: when discussing geopolitical economic divisions, use "core/peripheral" instead of "Global North/South" as advised in KR-01 Dimension 1, where applicable.

## Emerging fronts

Key emerging dynamics and fronts of struggle directly related to these contradictions include:

- Anti-imperialist sovereign bloc (e.g., China-Russia-Iran dynamics)
- Multipolar financial realignment (e.g., de-dollarization trends, alternative payment systems)
- Climate justice movements vs fossil capital and unsustainable practices

## Introduction

- We are now entering a qualitatively new phase of world history, times not seen in 100 years.
- Significant global changes have emerged in the years since the Great Financial Crisis of 2008.
- This can be seen in a new phase of imperialism and changes in the particularities of eight contradictions.

## The eight contradictions

### 1. Moribund imperialism vs emerging socialism led by China — *Primary*

- China's rise presents a historic challenge to Atlantic imperialist powers.
- China overtook US GDP in PPP in 2013, without colonialism or conquest.
- The US has become more aggressive as China's influence grows.
- The US has subordinated European and Japanese bourgeoisie post-Merkel and the Ukraine war.
- Intra-imperialist contradictions weakened; the US pushed Japan and Germany to militarize.
- Europe's economy harmed by decoupling from Russia; the US benefits.
- Europe still tied economically to China; the US pressures decoupling.
- Triad (US-EU-Japan) structure consolidated; US bourgeoisie unified on foreign policy.
- World economic center shifting; Global South + Russia = 65% of GDP (PPP).
- US GDP share declining (from 27% in 1950 to 15% now); long-term decline in the rate of profit.

### 2. G7 imperialist ruling classes vs capitalist elites in the Global South — *Secondary*

- Post-1990s unipolarity wanes; new cracks in elite alliances.
- India's bourgeoisie needs trade with Russia; opposes US hegemony at times.
- The US cannot always fulfill demands of Indian, Turkish, Saudi elites.
- This contradiction remains secondary to the China vs G7 contradiction.

### 3. Popular classes of the Global South vs US-led imperial elite — *Primary*

- The West still enjoys soft power across classes in the Global South.
- Rising resistance: youth protests in West Africa, Colombia's new government, environmental activism.
- Working-class women, youth, and the poor are increasingly anti-imperialist.
- Growing rejection of Western hypocrisy on human rights and environmental destruction.
- US hybrid wars and sanctions increasingly exposed.

### 4. Finance capital vs investment and development needs of society — *Secondary*

- Falling profit rate prevents sufficient reinvestment in the real economy.
- Investment crisis in the capitalist world, especially outside Asia.
- Hedge funds and private equity dominate; $9.8 trillion in private assets.
- Derivatives speculative bubble: $18.3T market, $632T notional.
- US tech monopolies (Google, Facebook, Amazon) extract rents and support soft power.
- The military-industrial complex also attracts surplus capital.
- Public investment has collapsed; infrastructure neglected.
- Tax evasion and public-sector privatization damage the global working class.
- Global South debt crisis manipulated for rent extraction.
- Permanent unemployment and precarity rise; inequality and despair grow.

### 5. Popular classes vs domestic elites in the Global South — *Secondary, from a global perspective*

- Country-specific manifestations; peaceful resolution in socialist countries.
- In capitalist-aligned states, elites dominate wealth; widespread poverty.
- Pro-West middle class impedes mass access to power.
- Local bourgeoisie uses class hegemony to suppress majoritarian interests.

### 6. US-led imperialism vs sovereign nations — *Primary*

- Four types: socialist, progressive, sovereignist, and Russia.
- The US employs hybrid war: sanctions, invasions, propaganda.
- Russia is uniquely targeted due to its history and resources.
- The US aims to dismantle Russia's nuclear capacity and sovereignty.

### 7. Discarded working class in the Global North vs domestic bourgeoisie — *Secondary, from a global perspective*

- Signs of discontent, but fragmented by racism and propaganda.
- White supremacy used to block class unity.
- Public anti-imperialism in the North has sharply declined.

### 8. Western capitalism vs the planet and humanity — *Primary*

- Capitalism threatens ecological and human survival.
- Rejects planning and peace; pushes nuclear conflict.
- The Global South offers the possibility of peace and ecological harmony.

## Conclusion

- The US global empire is entering decline.
- Neoliberalism is collapsing under internal contradictions and injustice.
- New movements give hope for a humane alternative to capitalist torment.
